Methodology

How flows are simulated, what each quality-control layer reports and how the indicators are calculated.

The site simulates walking trips in Brussels on OpenStreetMap data. The flows show where incomplete mapping weighs most on the simulated trips. The quality-control layers point to topology errors or missing tags on specific objects, with a link to the editor.

Routing costs

The router (Dijkstra, igraph) looks for the path with the lowest weight. The weight of a segment is its length multiplied by a factor that depends on the type of way: the higher the factor, the more readily the router takes a detour.

Quality-control layers

Flows on roads

Roadway segments where the simulation sends many pedestrians although no pedestrian infrastructure is recorded (mapped sidewalk or sidewalk tag). Check on the ground or on imagery whether a sidewalk exists, then add the tag or the geometry.

Sidewalk on one side only

For each road segment, roughly parallel highway=footway ways are looked for on either side. A highway=cycleway track with foot=yes, designated or permissive also counts as a sidewalk. The segment is flagged when a separate sidewalk is found on one side only.

Sidewalk tags

Status of the sidewalk, sidewalk:left, sidewalk:right and sidewalk:both tags on each road: separate, yes, documented on both sides, on one side only, or no tag.

Missing crossings

highway=crossing nodes on a road that has a parallel footway=sidewalk on both sides, but that are not connected to these sidewalks by a footway=crossing way. Three cases:

  • missing_way: no footway touches the node;
  • missing_tag: a footway touches the node but lacks footway=crossing;
  • wrong_tag: the crossing way is tagged footway=sidewalk.

In all three cases the sidewalks are mapped but the routing graph is cut at this point. Nodes on service, cycleway or track, and crossing:continuous=yes crossings, are not flagged as missing_tag.

Crossing without footway=crossing

Short footways that connect a sidewalk on each side of a road without carrying footway=crossing. These are candidates: check them before editing.

Kerbs and tactile paving

On each crossing, the barrier=kerb nodes at both ends of the footway=crossing way are examined. A point is placed when the kerb is missing or has no tactile_paving. An inconsistency is flagged when the crossing's tactile_paving contradicts that of the kerbs: yes or no expects the same value on both sides, incorrect expects one side yes and the other no. Two footway=crossing ways end to end are treated as a single crossing.

Disconnected islands

Parts of the pedestrian network with no link to the main network. A “missing junction” almost touches the network (2 m at most) and probably lacks a shared node. An “isolated island” has nothing nearby, often because of private access, a gate or incomplete mapping. The layer also flags steps whose level does not overlap that of a connected way; in multi-level stations this sometimes produces false positives.

Indicators

The completeness score on the Statistics page is the average of five shares, as percentages:

  • road segments whose sidewalks are documented by a tag (separate, yes or both sides; one side only counts for half);
  • road segments whose sidewalks are mapped as separate ways;
  • highway=crossing nodes connected to a footway;
  • highway=crossing nodes connected to a way correctly tagged footway=crossing;
  • length of road with a sidewalk detected on both sides, among roads with at least one.

The Trends page also tracks the presence of surface and smoothness on footways and of tactile_paving on crossings.

Contributing

  1. Spot a red segment (flow on a road) or a point from a quality-control layer.
  2. Click it to open the object in iD or JOSM, or use “Edit in OSM” from zoom 16.
  3. Fix the tags or the geometry.
  4. The map is recomputed every night from the day's OSM extract.
